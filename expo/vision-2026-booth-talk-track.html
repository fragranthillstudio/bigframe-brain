The method: four moves, ninety seconds, one part in hand

Every conversation on the stand follows the same four moves. The visitor almost always opens with "we have this application, can you do that?" The wrong answer is "yes". The right answer is a question that turns their application into a measurable feature, then a demo of that feature, then a promise with a date.

Move 1 · 10 s

Hook

One sentence that says what we are and why it matters: repeatability you can measure on this table, from 10 mm to 3.6 m.

Move 2 · 30 s

Qualify

Three questions, always the same: what part, what feature and tolerance, what happens today when it fails. Their answers choose the camera.

Move 3 · 40 s

Show

Walk to the camera their answers point to. Capture their part if they have it, ours if not. Show the number, not the picture.

Move 4 · 10 s

Commit

Lead card written in front of them: part, plant, feature, tolerance, test date. A date said out loud is a contract.

Three rules that decide the numbers.
  • Never say "accuracy" or "high precision". We say repeatability, and we say how it was measured (regional, 2σ, ceramic plate, 100 captures, page 22 of the catalog). German engineers respect this; it is the one sentence that separates us from the price players around us.
  • Never answer "can you do that?" with "yes". Answer with the feature question. A "yes" costs nothing and is worth nothing; a "we test your part next week" is worth a lead.
  • Every answer ends with an action. A capture, a page in the catalog, or a date on the lead card. Never end on information.

German on the stand: register, vocabulary, pronunciation

Register

  • Always Sie, never du, even with young engineers.
  • Short declarative sentences. Germans trust "Das ist 0,36 µm" more than "we believe this delivers exceptional performance".
  • No superlatives, no "best", no "revolutionary". Say the number and the method.
  • "Ehrlich gesagt" (honestly) before a no earns trust; use it once per conversation, not more.
  • Numbers: decimal comma, spoken "null Komma drei sechs Mikrometer".
